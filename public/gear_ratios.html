Gear ratios

How a tiny motor spinning 6,000 times a minute can lift a heavy robot arm, by trading speed for torque.

Speed (RPM)
How fast a shaft spins: revolutions per minute.
Torque (kg·cm)
How hard a shaft twists. 1 kg·cm holds 1 kg on a lever 1 cm long.

6,000 RPM

The rule in 3 steps

1
Find the ratio
Ratio = driven teeth ÷ driver teeth
Driver = pulley on the motor. Driven = pulley on the arm.
42 ÷ 14 = 3, written 3 : 1.
2
Speed goes down
Speed out = speed in ÷ ratio
6,000 ÷ 3 = 2,000 RPM.
The arm turns 3× slower.
3
Torque goes up
Torque out ≈ torque in × ratio
1.47 × 3 = 4.41, about 4.3 kg·cm after a little friction. 3× stronger.

Why does that work?

Same belt, same pull

The belt pulls on both pulleys with the same force. Torque is force times distance from the center:

Torque = Force × Radius

The big pulley's edge is 3× farther out, so the same pull gives 3× the twist. It's why a long wrench loosens a stuck bolt.

Same belt, same speed

Every belt tooth that wraps the small pulley must also wrap the big one. The big pulley has 42 teeth, the small one 14.

So the motor must turn 3 times to move the arm pulley around once.

No free lunch: power stays the same

Power = Torque × Speed. Gears can't make extra power. They only reshape it. Picture power as the area of a rectangle: the width is speed and the height is torque.

width = speed
height = torque
1 : 1 · fast and weak
3 : 1 · balanced
6 : 1 · slow and strong
All three rectangles have the same area. Gearing changes the shape, not the size. (Real gears lose a little to friction, so the area shrinks slightly.)

You already use this

Bike, low gear
Small front gear, big back gear. Your legs spin fast, the wheel turns slowly, and hills get easier.
Car, first gear
A big ratio gets a heavy car moving. Higher gears trade that torque back for speed on the highway.
Robot arm
At 1 : 1 the motor stalls under a heavy arm. Geared about 50 : 1, it has about 68 kg·cm to lift it, slowly.
Try it
A robot has a 12-tooth pulley on this motor and a 60-tooth pulley on its arm.
1. What is the gear ratio?
2. How fast does the arm spin?
3. About how much torque does it get?
Answers: 5 : 1 (60 ÷ 12) · 1,200 RPM (6,000 ÷ 5) · about 7 kg·cm (1.47 × 5 = 7.35, minus a little friction)
Remember: big pulley on the output = slower and stronger.
